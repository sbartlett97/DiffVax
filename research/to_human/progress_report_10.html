H1a Training Dynamics — A Paper-Worthy Finding

The 26-epoch H1a training run (converged but uncheckpointed) revealed a bimodal loss distribution that will appear in Section 3.2 of the paper:

FLUX batches (75%):
Loss₁ ≈ 0.8–1.3  ← FLUX is a harder adversary (12B DiT, stronger edits)
SD1.5 batches (25%):
Loss₁ ≈ 0.03–0.11  ← SD1.5 is easier; provides stable gradient signal
Loss₂ (perturbation reg): 0.952 → 0.005 (99.5% reduction over 26 epochs)

This alternating curriculum is analogous to GAN training dynamics: the high-capacity adversary (FLUX) drives strong perturbations; the low-capacity adversary (SD1.5) prevents mode collapse to FLUX-only perturbations. We hypothesize this is why H1a transfers to SD3.5 without training on it.
